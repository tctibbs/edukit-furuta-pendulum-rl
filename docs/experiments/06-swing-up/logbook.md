# Swing-up training runs

Log of every configuration tried, failures included. The reasoning behind the working one is in the [write-up](./writeup.md). Training host and software stack are [Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md)'s, pinned in [`config.yaml`](./config.yaml).

`Furuta-SwingUp-Direct-v0`, 1,024 environments, seed 1 until a configuration passes the gate. The gate, [`scripts/evaluate_swingup.py`](../../../scripts/evaluate_swingup.py), runs 256 hanging starts within 0.4 rad and 1 rad/s for 30 s. An episode reaches vertical at its first step inside 3 degrees and succeeds if its last 5 s stay inside 3 degrees with no travel termination.

## Premise

Run 1 starts from the actor of [Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md)'s a60d policy (`momentum_a60d_seed1_model_299.pt`). a60d catches the firmware's own swing-up 256 of 256 times in simulation and two of two on the bench. The action scale stays at a60d's 60 rad/s^2 ([ADR-0004](../../adrs/0004-two-actuator-paths.md)). At that bound the recoverable set is 5.15 rad/s of unstable mode. Every setting not in the table is a60d's, apart from the two defect fixes under Before training (the episode clock and the rate estimators' reset).

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Physics rate | 4 kHz | [Experiment 04](../04-model-reconciliation/writeup.md)'s replay of the golden trace (Experiment 03's mode `u` capture of the stock swing-up) rose 23 degrees higher at 4 kHz than at 1 kHz. a60d trained at 1 kHz |
| Episode length | 10 s training, 30 s gate | a60d's training length. A 10 s episode holds a swing-up and 5 s of hold, and puts three times as many from-rest openings in each batch as a 30 s episode would |
| Discount | 0.999, a 2 s horizon | a60d's, kept over the swing-up runner's default of 0.9998. That default is a 10 s horizon, set for a swing-up at 11.78 rad/s^2 that takes about 15 s, and at 60 the swing-up takes a few seconds |
| Value loss | unclipped | a60d's is PPO's clipped value loss. The clip is 0.2 in absolute return units, and returns here run in the thousands |
| Iterations | 600 | Judgment. a60d took 300 for the hold alone |
| Travel termination | pi in training and in the gate | a60d trained at 4 pi and was scored at pi |
| Fall-over termination | none | Hanging is where every episode starts |
| Action bound | `tanh` on the command | Under a clamp the policy's mean can drift far outside the bound with no change in the command |
| Upright term | `cos(tilt)` | Proportional to the height the rotor pumps through. a60d's quadratic is flat across everything but the last 20 degrees |
| Energy term | `2.0 * (1 - abs(E - E_up))`, normalized to 0 hanging and 1 on the separatrix | New. The one term that pays for reaching the top and not for average height |
| Capture well | 2.0, quadratic inside 20 degrees | a60d's quadratic upright term at twice its weight, kept inside 20 degrees beside the cosine. Supplies the gradient where the cosine has none |
| Pendulum-rate charge | -0.0096, inside 20 degrees only | a60d charges -0.005 at every tilt. This is the swing-up task's default -0.25 scaled by (11.78 / 60)^2. A marginal catchable arrival then costs 0.26 per step against the well's 2.0, as it did at 11.78. Left at -0.25 it costs 6.63, and the policy is paid to stay away from the top |
| Resets | 40 percent hanging, 25 percent near vertical across the recoverable set with the arm moving, and 35 percent spread over the circle at up to 10 rad/s | The near-vertical share uses a60d's recoverable-set spread |

## Before training

**Defect, the episode clocks in step.** From its first commit the trainer passed `init_at_random_ep_len=False` to the runner, with no reason recorded; Isaac Lab's corresponding script passes True. Every environment then started its episode on the same step, a task that rarely terminates kept them there, and every 128-step rollout was the same quarter second of every episode. The opening the gate scores reached only the updates that spanned it. The trainer now passes True.

**Defect, the near-vertical reset share.** The near-vertical share, meant to teach the catch, was drawn from the balance task's original spread, 2 degrees and 0.25 rad/s with the arm within 0.5 rad and nearly still. In [Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md) a policy trained on that spread held 16 of 236 scripted arrivals, and one trained across the recoverable set with the arm displaced and moving held 166. A swing-up arrives fast with the arm mid-stroke, and the only catchable arrivals the policy saw were the roughly 5 percent of the wide share's resets that land inside the recoverable set with a still arm. The share now draws the momentum task's spread, tilt across +/-0.3 rad, unstable mode across +/-0.9 rad/s scaled by the acceleration bound (+/-4.58 rad/s at 60 rad/s^2) with the rate following, and the arm across +/-2 rad and +/-6 rad/s (`src/furuta/env.py` and the task config).

**Defect, the trace recorder's acceleration bound.** `scripts/record_swingup_trace.py` had no `--accel-bound` and would have replayed a checkpoint trained at 60 rad/s^2 at 11.78 rad/s^2, every command a fifth of what the policy meant. The evaluator had the same defect, and it was fixed on 2026-09-07 before the first gate ran. The recorder now takes the flag and also writes the arm speed.

**The critic could move 0.2 per update.** The trainer uses PPO's clipped value loss: the value prediction is clamped to within `clip_param` of the value recorded at rollout time, the loss is the larger of the clipped and unclipped squared errors, and `clip_param` is 0.2 in absolute return units. Once the critic's error exceeds 0.2, the clipped branch has the larger loss and its gradient is zero. The critic can then close on its target by at most 0.2 per update. Balance returns run near 2,500, and a policy that starts pumping moves its returns by thousands in a few iterations. The balance task did not escape the clip either, and a60d's committed checkpoint values a still upright start at 804 against a fixed point near 1,500 to 2,000 under its own reward and discount. Its critic was still about half short after 300 iterations, and the policy caught anyway. A critic half short on a task whose returns move slowly is survivable; one that cannot follow returns moving by thousands may not be. Nothing measured that; it is a judgment. The clip is off here (`--no-value-clip`) and the loss is the plain squared error. The value loss in the log is the check.

**Defect, the rate estimators at reset.** Both rate estimators seeded their 16-sample windows flat at the reset angle, and a pendulum reset at 4 rad/s read a rate climbing from zero over the first 32 ms, a quarter of the unstable time constant. The board's balance glue had the same defect, and `observer_seed` fixed it there ([Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md)'s first hardware defect). The estimators now seed a ramp at the reset rate, and a body at rest still reads zero.

**Centrifugal coupling in the planar model.** The full equations carry a term the [kit manual](../../design-inputs/references/edukit-manual.md)'s planar model drops, the centrifugal coupling `kappa * arm_rate^2 * sin(tilt) * cos(tilt)`, with `kappa` the rod's share of the pivot inertia, 0.974 here. A pendulum on a turning arm is flung toward horizontal. The coupling stiffens the top's instability and softens the bottom's restoring pull to `omega_g^2 - kappa * arm_rate^2`, zero at an arm speed of 7.45 rad/s. An arm kept above that speed can hold the pendulum in a steady swing short of vertical at close to the separatrix energy, and the energy term would pay for a swing that never arrives. The term has been in [`src/furuta/planar.py`](../../../src/furuta/planar.py) since [Experiment 04](../04-model-reconciliation/writeup.md), off unless a caller passes the arm speed, and the golden-trace replay and free-swing fits the model was validated against ran with it off. The arm-speed charge starts at a60d's weight and runs 1 and 2 test whether that is enough. The gate now reports peak arm speed.

**From-hanging diagnostics.** Training means cover a reset mixture that is 60 percent not the task, and a run can log a mean tilt of 30 degrees without one arrival from rest. The environment now logs, over hanging starts at each episode's end, the peak energy as a multiple of the separatrix energy, the closest approach to vertical in degrees, and the fraction that reached 20 degrees.  A forming pump shows there inside a hundred iterations, and the arm-speed trap shows as peak energy above 1 with an approach that never closes.

## Scripted baselines

**Sizing on the planar model.** In [Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md)'s planar sweep the bang-bang pump adds more energy per half swing than the recoverable set is wide at every bound above 25 rad/s^2. The energy pump of Åström and Furuta (2000) runs the bang-bang command at full magnitude until the energy passes `1 - 1/gain` of the way to the separatrix and scales it by `gain * (1 - E / E_up)` after. The kick pump is the firmware's kick on the acceleration path, one rest-to-rest pulse of 0.393 rad (the firmware's 200 microsteps) against the swing at each pass through the bottom and nothing between. [`figures/planar_sizing.py`](./figures/planar_sizing.py) runs all three from rest, with the recoverable set scaled to each bound, on the [kit manual](../../design-inputs/references/edukit-manual.md)'s equations and again with the centrifugal coupling. The table shows the runs with the coupling, and [`simulation/planar_sizing.csv`](./simulation/planar_sizing.csv) has every row.

| 25 | never | 4.4 s, unstable mode 1.14 rad/s, peak arm speed 8.0 rad/s | 11.6 s, 21 kicks, unstable mode -0.68 rad/s, peak arm speed 3.2 rad/s |
| 40 | 1.4 s, unstable mode -3.24 rad/s, peak arm speed 10.7 rad/s | 2.3 s, unstable mode -1.65 rad/s, peak arm speed 10.7 rad/s | 5.9 s, 12 kicks, unstable mode 1.88 rad/s, peak arm speed 4.0 rad/s |

From 25 rad/s^2 up the kick pump arrives with the arm never past 0.4 rad or 4.8 rad/s, and at 60 rad/s^2 it hands over at an unstable mode of 0.36 rad/s. At 11.78 rad/s^2 the firmware's kick arrives only because the driver's position move steps the arm to 1.57 rad/s in one pulse, and the acceleration path cannot make that impulse. The energy pump arrives from 25 up with the arm at 8 to 13 rad/s, and at 60 it hands over at an unstable mode of 4.0 rad/s, the edge of the spread a60d trained on. The centrifugal coupling makes it arrive hotter at each of those bounds but sooner only at 60, and it turns the bang-bang pump's arrival off at 25 rad/s^2 and on at 40.

**The existence proof in the environment.** Each scripted pump runs at 60 rad/s^2 from the gate's hanging starts and hands over to a60d inside the recoverable set (5.15 rad/s of unstable mode) and 0.35 rad of tilt. The bang-bang and energy pumps, queued before the sizing had the centrifugal coupling, run first; the kick pump follows in `scripts/host/drive_swingup06r3.ps1`. If the kick pump arrives and a60d holds, the task is feasible in the learner's own action space at arm speeds the bench has seen. If nothing arrives, the ladder starts with the pump.

**Bang-bang at 60, in the environment, 2026-09-10.** The planar model says this law overshoots the top at 60. In the full plant it reaches within 3 degrees in 242 of 256 episodes at a median 0.98 s, hands over in 165, and a60d holds 19 of those. Because this task has no fall termination, every one of the 146 lost handovers ends on the pi travel limit. The per-episode states say why they are lost. The handovers arrive at a median 3.1 rad/s of unstable mode with the arm 1.7 rad out and turning at 6.9 rad/s, and the hold rate falls with the arm's speed rather than the pendulum's. a60d holds 45 percent of the 22 handovers with the arm between 2 and 4 rad/s, 18 percent between 4 and 6, and one of 99 exceeding 6. An arm 1.7 rad out at 7 rad/s has 1.4 rad of travel left to stop in and then catch a 3 rad/s arrival, and a60d spends it. The trace of environment 0 shows the rest. After the lost catch the pendulum goes over and the law goes on pumping a rotating pendulum to 37 rad/s for the rest of the episode. Arrivals exist at 60 in the policy's own action space, in under a second. The arm's state at arrival is what the catch cannot take. The arm's speed limits the catch, as the centrifugal-coupling entry says it limits the pump. And the planar overshoot prediction is wrong here. The sizing table's own rows warn of it once the arm-rate term is in. `sw06r_proof_bangbang.csv`, per-episode `sw06r_proof_bangbang_episodes.csv`, on the host.

**The energy pump at 60, in the environment.** Gain 8 behind the 2 rad governor hands over in 254 of 256 episodes at a median 2.17 s after three half swings, and a60d holds 53 of them, 21 percent end to end, nearly three times bang-bang's 7 percent. What decides the hold is how gently the pendulum arrives: 29 of 32 handovers under 1 rad/s of unstable mode are held, 17 of 113 between 1 and 2, 7 of 30 between 2 and 3, and none of 79 exceeding 3. The arm matters less here than for bang-bang. The taper leaves it at a median 1.7 rad/s at handover, and 46 of the 53 holds come from arrivals with the arm under 2 rad/s. The task is feasible in the policy's own action and observation space at 60, in about two seconds. And a60d's catch is about a fifth of the rotor's. Trained across a 4.58 rad/s spread of unstable mode, it holds arrivals inside roughly 1 rad/s and loses nearly everything past 2. The 5.15 rad/s window is the rotor's theoretical capacity, and a swing-up that hands over to a hold like a60d's has to arrive inside 1 to 2 rad/s. That is what the capture well and the gated rate charge are for. The planar model with the arm-rate term predicts this law arriving in 0.8 s at 4.0 rad/s of mode; the environment gives 2.2 s at a median 1.66. The term overstates the fling as the manual's model understates it, and neither is a substitute for the full plant. Median arm 1.73 rad out at handover, peak arm speed before handover 12.6 rad/s in the traced episode. The arm-speed risk stands. `sw06r_proof_energy8.csv`, `sw06r_proof_energy8_episodes.csv` on the host.

**The kick pump at 60, in the environment.** Pulses of 0.384 rad, quantized from the firmware's 0.393 to whole control steps, at each pass through the bottom, no governor. It hands over in 67 of 256 episodes at a median 6.5 s after 11 kicks, as the planar model says, and a60d holds 37 of the 67, 55 percent. The arm at rest at handover, 0.23 rad/s rms against median arm speeds of 1.7 for the energy pump and 6.9 for bang-bang, gives the kick pump the best catch rate of the three laws. What the planar model does not say is that 187 of 256 episodes end on the travel limit before any handover. The pulses are meant to alternate and cancel, and on this plant they do not quite. The arm walks. End to end the three scripted laws give 7, 21, and 14 percent; the arm-quiet arrival is the kick pump's lesson and the drift is its cost, and a governor on top of it would presumably fix the drift at the price of some of the quiet. `sw06r_proof_kick.csv`, per-episode `sw06r_proof_kick_episodes.csv` on the host.

**a60d's hold under `tanh`.** a60d trained under a clamp, and in run 1 the swing-up task's `tanh` brings its actor's largest commands to 0.94 of what it learned to give and a unit command to 0.76. The clamped replay of a60d through the firmware's swing-up catches 256 of 256. If the squashed replay catches fewer, run 1 starts from a weakened hold, and either its first hundred iterations are read with that in mind or it is rerun from an a60d retrained squashed (eleven minutes). Run on 2026-09-10 after run 1, the squashed replay catches and holds 256 of 256, and the unstable mode at handover is 0.99 rad/s RMS under both.

## Risks on the bench

All analysis so far is simulation, and the bound it trains at is a hypothesis about the motor ([ADR-0004](../../adrs/0004-two-actuator-paths.md)). A pump at 60 could meet conditions on the bench that a hold at 60 did not.

- **Arm speed.** The scripted energy pump sweeps the arm at 13 rad/s; the bench has demonstrated 7.9 rad/s in a hold and 5.4 in any capture with the pendulum caught. Stepper torque falls with speed, the arm's angle is dead-reckoned so a lost step is invisible, and the simulation's speed ceiling is a nominal 19.6 rad/s nobody measured. The measurement is the step-loss ladder [ADR-0004](../../adrs/0004-two-actuator-paths.md) names: open-loop acceleration-path strokes with the pendulum hanging, the pulse count and the pendulum's encoder both logged. A lost step shows as the arm not returning to its start count. It sets the speed clamp the simulation should carry. The kick pump's 4.8 rad/s is the regime to aim the learner at until then.
- **The centrifugal coupling.** Both models carry it now, but no bench trace has the arm faster than a kick's 2.65 rad/s while the pendulum swings. The measurement is the hanging pendulum's small-swing frequency with the arm turning at constant speed on the acceleration path, predicted as `sqrt(54.04 - 0.974 * arm_rate^2)`: 7.35 rad/s at rest, 6.9 at 2.65, 4.4 at 6, and the pendulum lifting on its own at 7.45.
- **The driven plant past horizontal.** The simulated firmware swing-up tracks the golden trace's half-swing peaks to about 105 degrees and then falls behind (20 kicks and 11.6 s against the board's 16 and 8.0 s), and it hands over at 1.07 rad/s where the board hands over at 1.4 to 3.1. A learned pump's bench arrivals would not match its simulated ones either. The measurement is the one [Experiment 04](../04-model-reconciliation/writeup.md) names, a swing-up capture with the arm's position logged through the kicks.
- **Amplification at the rail.** The machine multiplied a60d's settled command by 2.3 and a60c's ([Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md)'s a60d before its action-rate charge went from 0.01 to 0.5) by 3.9, and a pump spends its time at the rail. The first bench pump's command trace, its rail fraction, and the supply current are the reading, against the chatter statistics [Experiment 05](../05-balance/writeup.md) recorded.
- **The policy from rest on the board.** Mode `b` runs the stock kick loop first and hands over at the top. Running the 500 Hz policy loop from a settled pendulum needs a new mode. Building it is engineering work, and it gates any hardware result.

## Training runs

Each row changes one thing against the run its Change column names. These run numbers are not the `rN` in a run id (run 9 is `r7-smooth`; [checkpoints/](./checkpoints/README.md) maps them) or in a driver's file name (`scripts/host/drive_swingup06r15.ps1` trained run 9). Every run trains for 600 iterations and is scored at its final checkpoint. Runs 1 and 2 ask whether the balance half was the missing piece: the same configuration started from a60d's actor and from random weights. Only the actor is loaded. The checkpoint's critic predicts balance returns under balance's reward, its learned exploration std is the 0.25 of a settled hold, and its optimizer moments belong to that run; carried over they would drive the first updates before the new critic has fitted anything. All three start fresh and the std starts at 1.0. If run 1 arrives and run 2 does not, the hold was the half the learner could not find on its own and the rest of the ladder builds on the warm start. If neither arrives, the pump is the wall, the warm start was not the lever, and the from-hanging diagnostics say how far the pump got. If both arrive, the fixes made before training were the difference and the warm start is a convenience.

**Queued, 2026-09-10.** `scripts/host/drive_swingup06r.ps1` holds the existence proofs for the bang-bang and energy pumps, then runs 1 and 2, each with its gate. The training host is off the network for the first hour and the queue starts at 08:43.

**Defects, the actor-only loader and the exit code.** The loader unpacked the return of the policy's `load_state_dict` as a missing-and-unexpected pair; rsl_rl's policy overrides that method to return a bool. Run 1 died with a `TypeError` before its first iteration and exited 0. Kit's teardown returns 0 after a traceback. The driver trusted the exit code, spent 19 s scoring a checkpoint that did not exist (`FileNotFoundError`, exit 0 again) and went on to run 2. Run 2 never touches the loader and is valid. The loader now calls the base module's method directly. The experiment's four Kit scripts catch an exception at the top level, print it, and exit 1 through `close_and_exit`, and the driver reads each step's log for a traceback. `scripts/host/drive_swingup06r3.ps1` requeues run 1 with the kick pump proof and the `tanh` replay behind it.

**Run 2, the control, from random weights.** Gate: 236 of 256 reach within 3 degrees of vertical from rest, median 2.59 s and 90th percentile 2.84 s, 20 end on the travel limit at a median 0.53 s, none holds, peak arm speed 10.5 rad/s. The swing-up half of the task now exists after 58 minutes of training, and what it produces is an arrival the policy cannot stop. The training log says how it got there, in four samples of the from-hanging diagnostics. By iteration 120, 58 percent of hanging starts reach the capture well with a closest approach averaging 23 degrees and 1.56 of the separatrix energy. By 230 every one of them passes through vertical, closest approach 0.02 degrees, at 2.1 times the separatrix energy. The pendulum rotates over the top, collects the alive and energy terms, and glances the capture well on each pass. By 340 the capture term has climbed from 0.06 to 0.26, the peak energy has fallen to 1.46 and mean tilt to 72 degrees, and the policy spends more of each episode near the top; the exploration std has fallen from 1.0 to 0.27. By 448 it has lost ground, capture 0.17, the from-rest pump down to 0.56 of the separatrix and the closest approach out to 86 degrees, with the std at 0.23; by 564 it has recovered most of it, and it ends at capture 0.27, closest approach 14 degrees, 85 percent of hanging starts in the capture well, peak energy 1.22, std 0.22. The loop can learn a from-rest pump, and it learns it fast. It cannot turn the arrival into a hold. The energy in the capture well is a fifth higher than the separatrix, the near-vertical share's hold pays the capture term it does earn, and the exploration std has collapsed to a quarter by the time the pump is stable. The search for a gentle arrival is running on noise a60d's settled hold would have. The warm-started run 1, rerunning next, says whether an actor that can already hold changes the picture.

Reading `_apply_action` says where it comes from. The actuator integrates the command once per 2 ms control period into one position target, and `_apply_action` hands the drive that same target at every substep of the period (`src/furuta/env.py`, `set_joint_position_target` on `_rotor_target`). Under a stiff position drive the rotor therefore jumps to the new target in the first substep or two and holds for the rest of the period; the finer the substep, the sharper the jump. The real stepper moves the same 2 ms displacement as eleven microsteps spread across the period. The term that cares is the arm-rate coupling the planar sizing named. It goes as the square of the arm's speed, and a jump-and-hold profile that covers the period's displacement in a fraction `f` of the period has a mean squared speed `1/f` times the smooth motion's. At 4 kHz, with the jump inside 1 to 2 of 8 substeps, that factor is 4 to 8; at 16 kHz, 1 to 2 of 32, it is 16 to 32. The planar model integrates the smooth rate and matches the bench to 6 degrees; the articulation, fed a spiky one, pumps harder the finer it is stepped. The fix is to walk the drive target across the period's substeps as the microsteps do, and the test is the same replay: if the articulation with an interpolated target lands on the bench and stops moving with the rate, this was the plant defect the bench found. That change is a plant change. It gets an ADR, and every policy trains again on it.

**The bench replay with the drive target walked across the substeps.** `scripts/replay_trace.py` with `rotor_target_interpolation` on, attempt 1's rotor motion, at 2, 4, and 16 kHz: [simulation/replay_run0_smooth_2k.csv](./simulation/replay_run0_smooth_2k.csv), [simulation/replay_run0_smooth_4k.csv](./simulation/replay_run0_smooth_4k.csv), [simulation/replay_run0_smooth_16k.csv](./simulation/replay_run0_smooth_16k.csv). All three give the same pendulum: inside 90 degrees at 0.83 s against the bench's 0.84, bottom passes at 9.6 and 13.4 rad/s against 10.3 and 13.3, a mean difference from the measured tilt of 4.3 degrees over the first 1.5 s. The stepped target gave 17.1, 12.6, and 73.3 degrees at the same three rates and a first bottom pass anywhere from 6.4 to 14.0. The rate dependence is gone and the articulation sits on the machine, at 5.3 degrees rms against the planar model's 6.3. That is the plant defect the bench found, and the walked target is its fix. One position target per 2 ms held across every substep was a jump-and-hold arm, and the arm-rate coupling, which goes as speed squared, pumped the pendulum on the jumps. Walking the target across the substeps and giving the drive the actuator's velocity makes the simulated arm move as the microsteps do. The flag is on by default from here ([ADR-0006](../../adrs/0006-walked-rotor-target.md)), the old behavior stays reachable as `--stepped-rotor` for re-gating what was trained on it, and every policy trains again. [Experiment 04](../04-model-reconciliation/writeup.md)'s climb, which the golden trace could not settle, and its finding that the physics rate moved the driven replay by 23 degrees fit this defect. The jump got sharper with the rate, and the 4 kHz replay climbed higher. The golden trace has not been replayed on the walked target to test it.

## Bench sessions 3 and 4, 2026-09-11

**Session 3, firmware defects found in the morning, all in the mode `r` scaffolding and none in the policy path.** They cost about an hour and produced a run of results that looked like hardware failures and were not. First, the return-to-center added the night before called the driver's unbounded wait for a move to finish; after a reason 3 stop the move never completed, the board went silent after `RL DONE` with the motor energized, and the series died after one attempt. The wait is now bounded by `RL_RETURN_TIMEOUT_MS` (3 s) and the line reports `still moving` when it expires. Second, the fall stop fired the instant the pendulum passed 30 degrees after an arrival. It ended four of five attempts in [hardware/rl_swingup_20260911_control.tsv](./hardware/rl_swingup_20260911_control.tsv) 0.2 to 0.26 seconds after a genuine catch; the simulation's swing-up task has no fall termination at all and the policy recovers from falls routinely, and the fifth attempt of that same capture proves it on the bench by falling, re-pumping, and holding 26 s. The stop now needs the pendulum to stay outside 30 degrees for `RL_FALL_PERSIST_CYCLES` (3 s). That window still ends a rotation trap and gives a fall its chance. Third, that change put the new check at the head of the existing `else if` chain. The travel stop and the 30 s limit became branches of it and were skipped for the whole of any successful hold. A caught attempt ran past its time limit with the cable guard off and had to be stopped by reflashing. The three stops are independent checks now.

**Session 3, the dead-arm attempts.** Between 07:24 and 07:42 five attempts across two policies log the arm sweeping 4 to 6 rad of dead reckoning while the pendulum leaves hanging by 0.15 to 37 degrees. Replaying the first attempt in [`hardware/rl_swingup_20260911_session3.tsv`](./hardware/rl_swingup_20260911_session3.tsv) through the corrected plant, [`simulation/replay_session3_run0.csv`](./simulation/replay_session3_run0.csv), gives a pendulum 78 degrees off hanging against the machine's 0.15 degrees. The shaft does not move; the dead reckoning counts steps that never happen. The leading explanation, not proven, is the return-to-center defect leaving the arm further out on each attempt until, past about a turn, the encoder cable binds and the motor stalls. Whether the arm was wound is unobserved, and the same policies run correctly afterwards. This firmware cannot tell a stalled stepper from a policy that pumps and gets nothing. The return guards against a wound cable only when it completes, and three of nine returns logged in this experiment's captures end `still moving` with the position unchanged.
